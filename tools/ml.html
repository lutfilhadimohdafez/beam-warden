What it detects
The attack's own on-air signature — not its effect on the link

The BeamCraft attack works by injecting forged beamforming-feedback (BFI) frames that impersonate the victim. Those injected frames are themselves the giveaway: during an attack the victim's BFI appears on air far more often than the AP could be sounding it, and every forgery carries the RETRY=1 bit. So Beam-Warden does not look for the attack's effect on throughput (which barely moves on a strong, close link) — it detects the forgery storm itself.

The model, honestly
What we report vs. where the signal really is

We report a RandomForest (200 trees) scored with GroupKFold by phase (each 20-second A/B/A phase is one group, so near-identical adjacent windows never straddle the train/test split — a leakage-safe estimate). But the honest framing is that the detector is physics, not a 200-tree model: a single threshold on the doubled on-air BFI rate already separates every window. Dropping both RETRY features still gives 100% — an attacker who omits the RETRY=1 marker cannot inject forgeries without raising the rate, so they do not evade detection.

Per-environment calibration
The one production concept

Every network has its own normal BFI rate — a quiet home is not a busy event hall. So instead of a fixed number, Beam-Warden learns this network's normal first, then flags any window that spikes above it. You do that on the Learning tab: pick where you are, press Learn my normal Wi-Fi once with the attack off, and the live check then shows NORMAL or ATTACK for that place.

Results & honest status
What is proven, and what is still future work

100% detection (GroupKFold by phase, every fold 100%) on a real-hardware capture, well past the ≥ 55% OBJ3 target. Scope, stated up front: this is within one condition — one ~6-minute capture, two 20-second attack bursts, one distance / channel / traffic load. Cross-condition generalisation is untested and is the honest next step (≥ 3 captures across different days / distances / channels / loads, reported as held-out-capture accuracy). Packaging the engine as an on-box OpenWrt service is future work — detection needs a monitor-mode capture, which the AC2100's mt7615 can't expose alongside its AP, so today it runs on the laptop's monitor NIC beside the AP.
